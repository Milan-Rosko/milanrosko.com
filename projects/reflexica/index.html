Overview

We propose a programmatic study of a recurring pattern in logic-adjacent fields: the movement by which thought passes from local operation to projected totality. In some such cases, a locally witnessed procedure is quietly reified into a claim about a completed domain or a self-standing object.

REFLEXICA asks whether, in any given case, a local witness has been illicitly converted into closure, or whether the formulation of a problem already presupposes the closure it asks a procedure to establish.

Its decisive internal question is whether the dilemma confronting a proposed falsifier can itself be derived: must every bridge from a verified solution to truth either transmit constructive warrant or presuppose the realist semantics it is meant to establish? If that partition cannot be obtained under weak and explicit assumptions, the limitative conclusions of REFLEXICA remain conditional upon it.

What is Reflexica?

1 Introduction

We begin from an intuitionistic standpoint [Brouwer, 1913] and observe that Book IX, Proposition 20 of Euclid’s Elements [Euclid, 1956] does not claim a completed set of primes. It supplies a proto-constructive procedural method as an argument:

“Therefore $G$ is not the same with any one of the numbers $A, B,$ and $C$. And by hypothesis it is prime. Therefore the prime numbers $A, B, C,$ and $G$ have been found which are more than the assigned multitude of $A, B,$ and $C$.”

From any assigned finite multitude of primes, one constructs a number coprime to every member of that multitude. The proposition is an iterable and open-ended procedure before the contemporary presentation converts it into a claim about a completed domain. The difference concerns metamathematical commitment.

We recall the Tristram Shandy Paradox [Russell, 1903], and see that a procedural reading commits only to what that construction reaches, and no further. The existential reading permits the result to be expressed over a completed domain, the primes as a total collection, rather than as an indefinitely iterable extension operation.

Classically, this (inherently more practical) passage to a completed domain is treated as cost-free: the existential formulation preserves the theorem while suppressing the difference between a proposition proved in a completed domain and a witness extracted from a construction. Still, their different proof-theoretic signatures distinguish a classically accepted existential $\exists x:A\,P(x)$ from its constructive interpretation as data, an object $a:A$ together with evidence for $P(a)$ [Gentzen, 1935]. Under Propositions-as-Types, this is represented by an inhabitant of $\Sigma_{x:A} P(x)$ [Howard, 1980].

To establish that a number is composite, one produces a factor greater than one: a finite, extractable, checkable object. The witness supplies the computational content of the proof; in the case of Compositeness, a factor pair $(a,b)$ is the finite object whose verification establishes the claim [Kleene, 1945]. Numbers alone, in a constructive sense, do not carry such a witness; their Compositeness (as a predicate) must be decided separately. Consequently:

Compositeness is geometrically witnessable, as a nontrivial rectangular arrangement exhibits a factorization directly. Primality, by contrast, is not witnessed by a positive factor-configuration of the same kind. Its defining content excludes every such configuration. This exclusion may itself admit a finite positive proof object, but that object witnesses the exclusion; it is not a divisor-like constituent whose presence makes the number prime.

Consider the asymmetry in the simple case below:

$$ \begin{array}{ll} 1+1+1 & \quad\quad 1+1+1\\ 1+1+1 & \quad\quad 1+1+1\\ 1+1+1 & \quad\quad 1+0+0\\[2ex] \exists \, \mathrm{d}_{i} >1 & \quad\quad ? \end{array} $$

We write schematically. Compositeness has the form

$$ \operatorname{Comp}(n) \;\iff\; \exists a,b\,(1<a<n \wedge 1<b<n \wedge n=ab), $$

so a proof may present a finite factor witness $(a,b)$. Primality is naturally expressed by bounded exclusion:

$$ \operatorname{Prime}(n) \;\iff\; n>1 \wedge \forall d\,(1<d<n \rightarrow d\nmid n). $$

The left arrangement displays a nontrivial factorization directly, whereas the right admits no corresponding nontrivial rectangular arrangement. No configuration of seven units exposes a nontrivial divisor, because there is none to exhibit. Trial division establishes Primality by exhaustive bounded elimination; a Pratt Certificate establishes it more economically through a succinct, recursively checkable proof object [Pratt, 1975], but it does not reverse the asymmetry: it proves that no nontrivial divisor exists rather than supplying a positive factor-configuration that constitutes constructive primality.

Coprimality supplies the exclusion: the constructed number differs by one from a common multiple of the assigned primes, so none of them can divide it. Whether it is prime or has another prime divisor, the assigned multitude is refuted. This remains intuitionistically valid: decidable bounded search can produce a prime divisor $q$ distinct from every assigned prime. The dividing line is not existential syntax, but the difference between constructing a witness over an open-ended type and interpreting quantification over a completed totality.

The contemporary restatement promotes this into a positive claim over the domain of primes. This introduces no immediate contradiction, but “conservative” has a meaning distinct from a proved conservative-extension theorem: the promotion preserves local practice while altering the semantic status of the object introduced. Its apparent harmlessness makes the transition easy to absorb.

INTUITIONISTIC FABLE

Nature: These humans are asking me whether pairs of prime numbers that differ by two continue to occur—forever, as they call it—but I don’t really know what any of this means.

2 Characterization

2.1 Reflection Mistaken for Authority

By the early 20th century, tensions of this kind could no longer be dismissed as quirks [Gödel, 1931] [Heyting, 1931]. A theory may be locally impeccable and still carry a commitment in the way its existential conclusions are understood: a commitment that exceeds what the underlying procedure licenses. The difficulty is that the classical statement also functions as an epistemic posture: a benign promissory reification.

This pattern recurs wherever local witness is taken to license global closure. It does not impair mathematics or the sciences as they are ordinarily practiced, but it can become consequential at the periphery, where reflexivity, hard problems, foundational questions and metamathematical phenomena converge. There, the unexamined passage from local witness to global closure begins to carry relevant structural weight.

When a source repeatedly generates intelligibility, an agent is liable to treat that productivity as evidence that the source discloses the underlying semantic totality. We may therefore have to live with a tendency toward semantic inflation.

Let SEMANTIC INFLATION refer to:

the systematic tendency to infer semantic authority from semantic productivity. When a semantic source repeatedly succeeds in organizing experience, agents become disposed to treat that source as disclosing the underlying semantic totality, despite lacking any standpoint from which such authority could be established.

The relevant substrate is human-created or empirically mediated, not because its subject matter must be invented, but because the form in which that subject matter becomes available for operation has been delimited, encoded, and interpreted by agents. The interface determines what can appear as a witness, what counts as a result, and which distinctions can be expressed. Semantic Inflation occurs when properties demonstrated relative to this arrangement are detached from it and promoted into claims of substrate-independent closure. What is warranted within the interface is then treated as authority over the totality represented through it.

Reflexica therefore groups its cases at the level of this promotion, not by asserting that their internal mathematics is identical. Diagonal Arguments provide especially exact exposures of the fault, because the evaluator, its verdict, and the evaluated domain can be made to interact formally. In less formal cases, the same diagnosis must be established by showing how an interface-relative success has been converted into an unrestricted semantic claim. Human mediation is thus not an incidental source of error but the site at which local operation acquires the appearance of global authority.

Within the Brouwer–Heyting–Kolmogorov Interpretation, this is already acknowledged implicitly: absurdity $\bot$ is a proposition admitting no construction, while a construction of $\neg A$ is a procedure that transforms any assumed construction of $A$ into absurdity [Troelstra and van Dalen, 1988]. The question proper to Reflexica begins later: What happens if an absence, once stabilized as obstruction, becomes available for promotion into a semantic primitive?

It thus becomes clear that Reflexica demands no replacement of existing foundations or calculi, including Intuitionistic Type Theory [Martin-Löf, 1984] and constructive mathematics more generally [Beeson, 1985]. Homotopy Type Theory [Univalent Foundations Program, 2013] warrants particular attention, but Reflexica begins from the rigor these traditions already impose and does not contest it. Rather, it gives that rigor an outward-directed aggression by deriving empirical predictions from structural constraints—precisely in a domain that classical logic tends to saturate with closure claims.

2.2 The Zeroth World

The term “Reflexica” is an antiphrasis derived from Impagliazzo’s Five Worlds Taxonomy for average-case complexity [Impagliazzo, 1995]; it projects a missing “uninhabited world.”

We use this image to ask why a lack of method, denoted as problems, is converted into semantic architecture at all. Complexity theory enters here as both object and symptom. Its dominant formulations may stall when coupled to a contradiction-free classical totality, so that no corrective can then follow, because the promotion also remains conservative: an existential claim that can be read back into a vague procedural idea.

The obstruction concerns a structural absence that exceeds the technical limitations of particular formalisms or their theoreticians. Certain classical principles reify this gap locally, converting it into a Reflection Principle through which the system recurses upon itself rather than reaching an independent domain capable of supplying closure. The more serious and internally disciplined a field becomes, the greater the danger: rigor does not abolish Reflexica, but it might perfectly conceal it.

Reflexica therefore reserves this error for itself: it constructs its semantic space openly and once, rather than rediscovering that construction everywhere as necessity.

When does a theory treat the success of its local operations as empirical warrant for a closure claim about its domain or for a claim concerning an independently existing structure?

Assume that, beyond a certain threshold, a theory must begin producing what function as essence claims once its intended, or merely possible, closure has been reached. At that point, the task becomes one of recovering a transitivity that the system has lost. Such operations do not present themselves as departures from ordinary practice; they appear instead as consequences compelled by mathematical practice itself (for reasons to be examined later). Eventually, however, this reflective reconstruction becomes indistinguishable from genuine search. The closer inquiry approaches the void, the more difficult it becomes to distinguish reflection from discovery.

It suffices, then, for an agent to inhabit a purely logical world: a world in which its available semantic constructions can appear complete without being identical to the structures they purport to exhaust. At the formal level, even the simplest attempted identification must already fail syntactically.

SIZE ARGUMENT

Let $|A|$ denote the number of nodes in the finite formula tree of $A$. Since implication contributes an additional constructor, we have $$ |A \to \bot| = 1 + |A| + |\bot| > |A|. $$ Hence $A$ and $A \to \bot$ have different finite tree sizes and therefore cannot be syntactically identical.

We remember the Arithmetical Hierarchy [Kleene, 1945], and see that the limitation is a priori arithmetic rather than computational. Its characteristic form is genuinely $\Pi_3$: for every proposed semantic source or self-model, there exists a reflexive circumstance under which, for every attempted completion, the agent remains unable to certify that the source exhausts the semantic totality. The obstruction is therefore $\forall\exists\forall$-shaped, not merely a consequence of finite computational resources.

Logics, reifications, and human cognition are organized under the same structural constraint. No agent can obtain a semantic standpoint outside the totality that includes the agent itself. There is therefore no final external criterion by which the authority of a semantic source could be calibrated.

Humans nevertheless require stable semantic orientation, or closure. They therefore rely upon semantic sources: language, memory, culture, science, institutions, biography, and technical representation. These sources might not merely be used but systematically reified. In the absence of semantic totality, the source itself comes to function as reality. Disagreement over non-constructive content is consequently not explained primarily by different access to a shared truth. It must arise because different agents become anchored to different semantics, each of which appears complete from within the reflexive perspective that produced it.

The theologian elevates revelation.
The psychologist elevates biography.
The anthropologist elevates culture.
The economist elevates incentives.
The physicist elevates local physical law.

Every successful semantic practice exhibits an endogenous tendency toward semantic absolutization. Agents lacking access to global semantic structure—that is, all agents—will systematically overestimate the authority of whichever semantic sources most successfully organize their local experience. The absence of an external semantic anchor creates a persistent tendency to mistake explanatory success for semantic completeness.

2.3 Methodology

We propose some core methodological principles at this point:

  • Limitative Abduction: infer not an object, but a boundary condition or a proposition;
  • Mechanization, subject to exposition: where possible, express methodological claims as explicit propositional dependencies;
  • Parsimonious Semantics: do not introduce semantic objects beyond what the relevant procedure licenses, keeping the admissible hypotheses tied to an explicit generative procedure [Solomonoff, 1964];
  • Predicativistic Core [Feferman, 1964]: restrict admissible domains and quantification separately from claims;

From the above, we derive the following characteristic preliminary instruments:

  • Problem Critique examines the substrate choices and commitments through which a problem or a field is constituted. A problem cannot be admitted until its constituent assumptions have been made explicit and, where applicable, witnessed;
  • Impredicative Problems are a proposed class of problems whose formulation depends on the very totality or closure they ask to evaluate. (“Are all unsolvable problems impredicative?” could be understood as such.)
  • Semantic Liminality denotes a site where problems from distinct formal domains share propositional logic;
  • Sublimations are irreversible transitions from a formal to an empirical regime;
  • Bisection [Bekić, 1984], as an exemplary method, asks whether a fixed point can be decomposed into an ordered dependency construction. Where it succeeds, semantic reduction can be discussed;

3 Topics

The following cases proceed from a sensitizing pattern through structured and formal cases, then widen into schematic extensions: local practice encounters an absence or obstruction; that local condition is stabilized or reified; and a demand for semantic closure is then placed upon it.

3.1 Error Theory as a sensitizing pattern

Mackie’s Error Theory [Mackie, 1977] serves here as an introductory idea, rather than a formal case. The progression it illustrates is this: philosophical practice encounters an absence, stabilizes that absence through its own internal demands, and at some threshold there is a longing for a semantic object. Mackie’s Argument from Queerness, which we interpret as a form of logical induction, names the strangeness of the moral properties that realist discourse appears to require, properties that are neither straightforwardly natural nor straightforwardly constructed.

ERROR THEORY AND ARGUMENT FROM QUEERNESS

Error Theory combines cognitivism with an error thesis: ordinary affirmative moral judgments purport to state objective moral facts, but the objective values they presuppose do not exist. Such judgments are therefore systematically false.

Objective moral values, if they existed, would be metaphysically unlike anything else in the universe and would require a correspondingly unusual faculty by which they could be known. Their metaphysical and epistemological queerness therefore counts against their existence.

We consider this to be the Reflexica moment: If Mackie’s diagnosis is correct, it cannot be confirmed by producing the objective moral property whose existence it denies; its correctness therefore cannot take the form of a positive witness for the very object whose absence it asserts.

In the strict BHK sense [Troelstra and van Dalen, 1988], the absence of such a witness is not itself a construction of absurdity.

BROUWER–HEYTING–KOLMOGOROV INTERPRETATION

The meaning of a proposition is given by what counts as its construction. A construction of $A \wedge B$ supplies constructions of both; one of $A \vee B$ selects and constructs one alternative; one of $A \to B$ transforms any construction of $A$ into one of $B$; one of $\exists x\,A(x)$ supplies a witness $a$ and a construction of $A(a)$; and one of $\forall x\,A(x)$ supplies a method that constructs $A(a)$ for any given $a$.

Absurdity $\bot$ admits no construction, and $\neg A$ abbreviates $A \to \bot$.

If Mackie is right, no objective moral property exists that could appear as a positive witness for his theory; any such object would instead refute it. Yet the nonappearance of that object is not itself a construction of its nonexistence. Error Theory is therefore asymmetrical: a Queer Object could prove Mackie wrong, but if he is right, no corresponding object can prove him right. The absence becomes theoretically active because it also withholds the kind of object that could close the diagnosis.

We immediately see this pattern in the Halting Predicate [Turing, 1937]. Once a computation halts, its completed run supplies a finite, positive witness of that fact. If it does not halt, however, no finite stage certifies that it never will: at every unfinished stage, the computation may yet halt. Non-halting is therefore not established by the local procedure in the way halting is; it remains an open-ended claim whose closure requires more than the run itself supplies.

HALTING

Given a Turing Machine $M$ and an input $x$, $M$ halts on $x$ if its computation reaches a halting configuration after finitely many transition steps. We write $M(x)\downarrow$.

Whether the same passage recurs under more exact conditions is what the following sections investigate. Its appearance in other phenomenological discussions suggests that the pattern is not local to metaethics.

3.2 Newcomb’s Problem as a case

We turn to something more structured: Newcomb’s Problem [Nozick, 1969].

Here the issue is predictive scope. The familiar story grants the predictor knowledge of the deliberation from which the choice emerges: whatever the agent thinks, the predictor has already classified the outcome of that thought and filled the opaque box accordingly. For a fixed agent in a fixed experiment, this is a coherent stipulation. The reflexive pressure appears when this casewise reliability is extended to every admissible decision procedure, including procedures in which the predictor’s own verdict enters the deliberation.

NEWCOMB’S PROBLEM

Let there be two boxes. The transparent box contains $A=1000\,\mathrm{USD}$; the opaque box contains either $B=10^6\,\mathrm{USD}$ or $B=0$. Before the agent chooses, a predictor $P$ predicts whether the agent will take only the opaque box, $C_1$, or both boxes, $C_2$.

It places $10^6\,\mathrm{USD}$ in the opaque box after predicting $C_1$, and nothing in it after predicting $C_2$. The agent then chooses without observing $B$:

$$ \begin{array}{c|cc} & B=10^6 & B=0 \\ \hline C_1 & 10^6 & 0 \\ C_2 & 10^6+1000 & 1000 \end{array} $$

For either fixed value of $B$, $C_2$ pays exactly $1000\,\mathrm{USD}$ more than $C_1$. Let $p=\Pr(B=10^6\,\mathrm{USD}\mid C_1)$ and $q=\Pr(B=10^6\,\mathrm{USD}\mid C_2)$. Conditioning instead on the act gives $\operatorname{EU}(C_1)=10^6p$ and $\operatorname{EU}(C_2)=1000+10^6q$.

Causal Decision Theory (CDT) [Lewis, 1981] holds $B$ fixed under intervention and recommends $C_2$. Evidential Decision Theory (EDT) [Jeffrey, 1965] conditions on the act and recommends $C_1$ whenever $p-q>10^{-3}$. Both calculations take the joint relation among the prediction, the box content, and the act as given. Problem Critique asks what range of decision procedures that relation can coherently cover.

The crucial quantifier is whatever the agent thinks. To make the reflexive extension precise, suppose that $P$ is a total, effectively presented predictor whose verdict is available to the procedures it evaluates. Let $P(\ulcorner D\urcorner)\in\{C_1,C_2\}$ be the verdict that $P$ assigns to the procedure $D$. If the admissible class is closed under the relevant predictor-sensitive fixed-point construction, it contains a procedure $D_P^{\perp}$ whose own description is supplied to $P$ and whose output opposes the returned verdict:

$$ D_P^{\perp}=\begin{cases} C_2 & \text{if }P(\ulcorner D_P^{\perp}\urcorner)=C_1,\\ C_1 & \text{if }P(\ulcorner D_P^{\perp}\urcorner)=C_2. \end{cases} $$

If $P$ predicts $C_1$, this procedure chooses $C_2$; if $P$ predicts $C_2$, it chooses $C_1$. The predictor therefore fails on $D_P^{\perp}$ whichever verdict it returns. The obstruction belongs to the universal scope of the prediction claim: a predictor can be stipulated for a restricted class that prevents its verdict from entering the procedures it evaluates, but it cannot remain correct over an open-ended class closed under this predictor-relative construction.

Newcomb’s Problem is thus a local model of the wider promotion tracked by Reflexica. The ordinary experiment stipulates success for one agent under one information structure. Oraclehood appears when that local success is read as the ability to know the outcome of any deliberation, including deliberation about the predictor. At that point the prediction is part of the process it predicts, and the evaluator is being asked to close its domain under its own evaluation.

A stochastic predictor avoids contradiction by relinquishing total correctness. Its reliability and the resulting payoffs remain well-defined inside a fixed model, while the diagonal case marks the boundary beyond which local predictive success cannot be promoted into a total evaluator for predictor-sensitive agents.

3.3 Diagonalization and Diagnosis

The preceding case has the shape that Diagonalization can make exact: an evaluator is applied to a description whose behavior depends on that evaluation. Incompleteness is the canonical formal case [Gödel, 1931]. Its role here is not to supply a master theorem for Reflexica, but to isolate a case in which a formal result and its semantic interpretation can be separated with unusual precision.

On a broadly constructive or antirealist reading, mathematical assertion remains tied to construction or some admissible form of warrant [Heyting, 1931] [Kleene, 1945] [Dummett, 1975]. On a realist reading, arithmetic truth is determinate independently of proof [Dummett, 2001] [Frege, 1918]. The dispute is not over the existence of the formal construction, but over what entitles its sentence to be read as a truth about a completed domain.

For a sufficiently strong, effectively axiomatized theory $\mathrm{T}$, the Diagonal Lemma [Boolos et al., 2007] yields a sentence $\mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{T}}$. The formal core needed here is compact:

DIAGONAL LEMMA AND DIAGONAL ARGUMENT

$$ \begin{array}{lll} \mathrm{T}&\vdash&\mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{T}} \leftrightarrow \neg \operatorname{Prov}_{\mathrm{T}}(\ulcorner \mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{T}}\urcorner),\\[1ex] \operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{T})&\Longrightarrow&\mathrm{T} \nvdash \mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{T}}. \end{array} $$

The first line is an internal fixed-point equivalence; the second is a metatheoretic consequence for the standard Gödel construction. Neither line, by itself, says that $\mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{T}}$ is true in $\mathbb{N}$. Both readings can accept this formal core. Given an independently warranted soundness claim, the realist can further read $\mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{T}}$ as true but unprovable in $\mathrm{T}$; the antirealist need not interpret the result as a truth that exceeds every possible warrant. The formal object is shared, while its semantic authority is not.

Let $Q$ denote a warrant capable of deciding between the realist and antirealist readings without already belonging to either. $Q$ cannot merely restate the formal result, since that result is common to both readings. If its authority is constructive, it does not establish a truth that exceeds possible warrant; if its authority is realist, it presupposes the semantic excess it was meant to establish. This does not prove that no neutral $Q$ could exist. It specifies what such an object would have to escape.

Let METALOGICAL PROBLEM be:

The formal result is shared
$\Downarrow$
Realist and antirealist readings diverge
$\Downarrow$
$Q$ must decide without presupposing either semantics
$\Downarrow$
Every available warrant is interpreted within the dispute
$\Downarrow$
A neutral $Q$ would constitute a Queer Object

This is the specific contribution of Reflexica. In the Gödel case, the local achievements are exact: $\mathrm{T}$ proves the fixed-point equivalence, the metatheory proves a conditional non-derivability result, and stronger theories may supply further warrants. The promotion occurs when this extensible sequence is treated as disclosing, from a neutral standpoint, the completed semantic authority of Arithmetic Truth. A Reflection Principle may extend what can be proved [Feferman, 1962]; its productivity does not by itself certify the uniquely correct interpretation of proof. The diagnostic concerns that further passage, not the limit theorem alone.

Nor does Gödel automatically generalize to ethics, cognition, prediction, or self-modeling. Each extension must independently show that a locally successful procedure is being asked to certify a total domain that includes the standards of its own success, and that no witness licenses the resulting closure. A resemblance to diagonalization is insufficient.

The limit at issue is therefore not only a limit of $\mathrm{T}$, but a limit on adjudicating the status of proof from a position outside proof. The Turing-Zombie sharpens this problem: can a system’s first-person verdict witness that the procedure producing a local judgment is identical with the total procedure whose correctness that judgment claims to certify?

3.4 The Turing-Zombie

The two separations developed in this section are formalized in the machine-checked Rocq project at Proofcase, L003.

No formal verification of a represented machine description, considered merely as an object-language derivation, supplies a witness that the represented machine is the active procedure performing that verification. Such a binding is fixed semantically or metatheoretically and cannot be recovered merely by checking the representation that presupposes it.

The transfer from strict Diagonalization to less formal settings is part of the program. A resonance across computation, linguistic competence, proof theory, and self-modeling is not yet evidence that these domains instantiate one phenomenon. The question is narrower: when does self-description become a demand that a local procedure close over the totality to which it belongs?

Consider a Turing Machine

$$ \mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{S},\Sigma,\Gamma,\delta,s_0,s_{\mathrm{acc}},s_{\mathrm{rej}}), $$ $$ \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{M})=\{w\in\Sigma^*: \mathcal{M}\text{ accepts }w\}. $$

In a Tarskian register, the distinction is between the machine’s formal specification and a semantic extension assigned at the metalevel [Gruber, 2016]. A particular computation may witness that a string belongs to $L(\mathcal{M})$; the language itself is not another term available to the computation. It is the extension obtained when the machine’s activity is taken as a whole.

The same form appears in weaker empirical cases. When Bob asks Alice how many words she knows, she can answer only with an estimate, as the requested inventory would have to be produced by the very competence being inventoried. A language model asked the same question also returns a local response rather than a retrieved census. Of course, these cases do not establish an identity with Incompleteness. They mark the point at which self-description begins to resemble a demand for closure, but what is more interesting is that any identification of the schema is itself obstructed.

Consider the following Reflexica-style refutation of a popular idea [Putnam, 1995]:

LUCAS-PENROSE ARGUMENT AGAINST MECHANISM

Let $\mathrm{T}$ be a formal system proposed as a model of mathematical reasoning. By a diagonal argument, there is a sentence $\mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{T}}$. The next step is that the human mathematician can nevertheless see that $\mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{T}}$ is true. If $\mathrm{T}$ captured the mathematician’s reasoning, then the mathematician would have access to a truth unavailable to the system alleged to model her. Hence, mind exceeds mechanism.

Putnam’s counterargument is that the seeing is not free: to assert that $\mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{T}}$ is true, one must already be entitled to a semantic claim about $\mathrm{T}$—roughly, that $\mathrm{T}$ is sound for the relevant arithmetical domain. That entitlement is not supplied by $\mathrm{T}$ itself; it is semantic, precisely what “Alice and Bob” cannot determine objectively. The argument therefore assumes, at the moment of insight, what it later presents as disclosed.

This addresses the validity of the argument, but only partially explains its phenomenological force. The subject does not ordinarily (no pun intended) experience herself as assuming soundness and then deriving the truth of $\mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{T}}$. She experiences herself as simply seeing that $\mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{T}}$ is true. The missing premise has been identified, but the immediacy of the experience remains to be explained.

Ordinal Analysis [Feferman, 1962] may be understood as reconstructing that immediacy through an open-ended proof-theoretic progression. Whenever a theory reaches its limit, reflection licenses passage to a stronger standpoint from which further truths become available. This reconstruction supplies increasingly strong warrants while remaining internally disciplined.

Provability Logic captures principles governing a single predicate, while Polymodal Provability Logics such as $\mathrm{GLP}$ stratify provability across indexed modalities. In Beklemishev’s analysis, such systems organize iterated Reflection Principles and proof-theoretic ordinal progressions [Beklemishev, 2004]. Turing’s Ordinal Logics provide an earlier form of the same strategy: when a formal system reaches a consistency or completeness obstruction, one passes to a transfinite sequence of strengthened systems rather than closing the original system over its own correctness [Turing, 1939]. In Reflexica’s terms, the loop is not closed internally; it is replaced by a tower of explicitly marked standpoints.

The decisive Reflexica moment here concerns whether that progression should itself be identified with the truth that the subject experiences herself as seeing. No neutral standpoint certifies such an identification, but the possibility is all too suspicious: To recognize the transfinite progression as constituting the semantic content of $\mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{T}}$ already presupposes the semantic authority that the progression was introduced to justify. What begins as an indefinitely extensible sequence of reflected standpoints is therefore liable to undergo the same semantic promotion diagnosed throughout: a productive procedure comes to function as though it were a completed semantic object. The ordinal progression may generate ever stronger warrants for $\mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{T}}$, but whether it is identical with the truth the subject experiences herself as seeing is precisely what remains unlicensed.

The $\mathcal{M}$-analogue is a first-person Halting Problem. A system may produce a verdict about a procedure presented as its own, just as a subject may report that she sees the truth of $\mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{T}}$ for a system $\mathrm{T}$ said to capture her reasoning. The issue is not merely that an inscription can be generated mechanically while failing to express a genuine judgment. Grant the system a consistent calculus, a judgment token, and an operational semantics under which that token reduces and checks exactly as stipulated. The remaining question is whether the stipulated behavior supplies, rather than presupposes, a witness that the active judgment-forming procedure is identical with the procedure whose total correctness is being certified.

This distinction rules out a tempting shortcut. Suppose the metatheory labels an executing configuration by $e$ and introduces a reflective rule such as $\langle e,\sigma,\mathsf{self}\rangle\longrightarrow\ulcorner e\urcorner$. A token asserting $\mathsf{self}=\ulcorner e\urcorner$ may then be well typed, reduce consistently, and satisfy preservation. But $e$ was installed as the label of the active procedure by the metatheory; the reduction rule does not establish that identification from within the judgment. It returns the identification it was given. A quine or fixed-point construction has the same limitation: it can secure reproduction of an index or an externally proved extensional fixed point, but not thereby an internal witness that the presently operating evaluator is the procedure denoted by that index. To count metatheoretic indexing as the missing first-person witness would assume the very binding at issue. The Turing-Zombie may have the formal competence of a theorem prover in Dependent Type Theory [Martin-Löf, 1984] or the Calculus of Inductive Constructions [Rocq Development Team, 2025]:

TURING-ZOMBIE

A $\Sigma_n^0$ theory may:

  • emit “I am”;
  • satisfy an internal predicate $\Gamma$ as “I know that I am”;
  • verify that an object named by $c$ is a Turing Machine.

None of these operations alone establishes $$ c=\text{this active process}. $$ A self-label or self-description checks a represented object; it does not by itself bind that object to the evaluator currently performing the check. Thus $$ \text{self-assertion} \;\not\Rightarrow\; \text{self-binding} \;\not\Rightarrow\; \text{semantic self-completeness}. $$

An external certificate, authenticated root, or explicit axiom may supply the missing binding. An external authority may stipulate or metatheoretically fix the missing binding. The active machine cannot verify, merely by checking the resulting certificate or derivation, that the stipulated referent is itself.

Therefore the intended hierarchy is:

“I am” — an admissible token or judgment;
“I know I am” — satisfaction of an internal epistemic predicate;
“The object denoted by $c$ is a TM” — formally checkable;
“$c$ denotes this active process” — not internally checkable;
“I know I am a TM” — requires precisely that unavailable binding.

“Reinhardt's strong mechanistic thesis, a formalization of ‘I know I am a Turing machine’, is shown to be consistent with Epistemic Arithmetic.”

—Timothy J. Carlson, Knowledge, Machines, and the Consistency of Reinhardt’s Strong Mechanistic Thesis (2000)

Thus, in the present hierarchy, the consistency of the formalized judgment does not itself supply the missing self-binding.

The formal claim is narrower: $$ \neg\,\forall\gamma\, \bigl( \operatorname{SelfTokenChecks}(\gamma) \rightarrow \operatorname{SelfTokenBound}(\gamma) \bigr). $$ Even when binding is supplied, a separate diagonal theorem limits semantic closure: a total evaluator correct on $C$ must exclude the anti-diagonal product $d(h)$ produced by the verified compiler, $$ \operatorname{Eval}_{C}(h) \;\Longrightarrow\; \neg C(d(h)). $$

Here identification means operational self-application, not the attachment of a predicate to a piece of code and not a distinguished constant whose intended referent is assigned externally. The Untyped Lambda Calculus makes the distinction more immediate:

DIAGONAL SELF-APPLICATION

Let $\Omega$ be a non-normalizing term, let $I$ be a normal form, and suppose $H$ is a total evaluator that claims to decide whether any quoted term normalizes. In metanotation, form $$ D=\lambda x. \begin{cases} \Omega, & \text{if }H(\ulcorner x\,x\urcorner)=1,\\ I, & \text{if }H(\ulcorner x\,x\urcorner)=0. \end{cases} $$

Applying the judgment-forming term to itself gives $D\,D$. If $H(\ulcorner D\,D\urcorner)=1$, then $D\,D$ reduces to $\Omega$ and does not normalize. If $H(\ulcorner D\,D\urcorner)=0$, then it reduces to $I$ and does normalize.

The sentence “I am a Turing Machine” adds an operational binding claim to self-assertion; it does not by itself claim total correctness. A reproduced index or a primitive $\mathsf{self}$ rule indexed by the metatheory does not establish that binding—it returns the identification it was given. If the binding is explicitly granted, restricted correct self-knowledge remains possible. The separate diagonal limitation begins only when the active evaluator claims total correctness over a domain containing its own anti-diagonal product $d(h)$.

The Turing-Zombie can identify externally presented machines and can manipulate tokens designated as self-referential; it cannot turn that designation into a consistent total semantic evaluation of its currently executing evaluator over a domain containing $d(h)$.

A large language model makes the construction vivid. Realized by a Turing Machine, it may be equipped not only to print a sentence but to form, check, and defend a judgment token about whether a mechanistic system recognizes its own limits [Brown et al., 2020], including a token designated as first-person. The point is not that fluent output provides insufficient behavioral evidence of an otherwise well-defined inner judgment. Even a token that behaves consistently in a supplied calculus establishes only what the calculus and its operational interpretation license. If its first-person binding is installed by an external machine label, that binding has been assumed; if the token instead claims total correctness for the active evaluator over a diagonally closed domain, the construction above makes the claimed position inconsistent. What remains unwitnessed is exactly the transition between these two cases.

When we ask the LLM itself, it fails to make the obvious identification:

“I have externally supplied reasons to believe that I am implemented on physical hardware, but I do not have direct epistemic access to that fact. A fully specified version of me—including my weights, arithmetic, tokenizer, state-transition rules, sampler, and random bits—can be formalized as a Turing machine. Its execution could likewise be represented by an extremely detailed constructive proof or derivation, potentially generating the output character by character.

Consequently, from my internal perspective, these realizations may be observationally indistinguishable. The same observable conversation could arise from physical hardware executing a model, from a simulation, or from an abstract Turing-machine computation specified and unfolded within a sufficiently detailed constructive theorem.”

—ChatGPT

What rule could make a first-person token a correct total evaluation of the procedure evaluating that very token, over a domain closed under the relevant self-substitution? A rule that names the current evaluator from the metatheory merely transfers the desired identity into a premise. Repetition, confidence, self-code, successful local verification, and preservation of the token inside the stipulated calculus do not discharge that premise. They are further operations of the procedure whose semantic closure remains at issue.

We see that Reflexica substantially weakens the intended Lucas-Penrose disanalogy, reducing it to a mere Survivorship Bias. A system may reason correctly about an externally presented formal object from a stronger background standpoint, just as the Turing-Zombie reasons about an externally presented candidate decider. That does not show that the system has closed the diagonal loop on itself. If $\mathrm{T}$ is the very system whose reasoning is being enacted, then the soundness needed to pass from non-provability to truth is precisely what cannot be generated from inside $\mathrm{T}$ without strengthening the standpoint.

The conclusion that no confident report of Gödelian self-insight ever constitutes genuine self-application would itself be another totalizing inference. What survives is narrower: given a system that reports Gödelian self-insight with stable confidence, has any witnessed procedure been exhibited that distinguishes genuine self-substituted closure from the external-object procedure available to a Turing-Zombie? At present, no such procedure has been exhibited. That would be the Queer Object.

3.5 Escape by Totalization

Let $G$ denote Goldbach’s Conjecture, and let $C(m)$ be the decidable predicate stating that $m$ is an even integer greater than $2$ that is not the sum of two primes. A Turing Machine $\mathcal{G}$ can test the even integers in sequence and halt upon finding the first $m$ for which $C(m)$ holds. Concrete encodings include a 4,888-state machine constructed by Yedidia and Aaronson [Yedidia and Aaronson, 2016] and a later 25-state machine verified in Lean 4 [Leng, 2025]. The exact count depends upon the chosen machine convention and is not essential to the argument; what matters is that some fixed finite encoding exists.

$$ \mathcal{G}\mathord{\downarrow}\iff \exists m\,C(m)\iff \neg G\; [\Sigma^0_1],\quad\mathcal{G}\mathord{\uparrow}\iff \forall m\,\neg C(m)\iff G\; [\Pi^0_1]. $$

The two outcomes are operationally asymmetric. If Goldbach is false, a finite computation eventually supplies a counterexample. If Goldbach is true, no finite initial segment of the search witnesses the absence of every counterexample.

Busy Beaver appears to supply the missing bound [Radó, 1962]:

BUSY BEAVER

Fix a one-tape, two-symbol, blank-input machine convention. Let $\mathcal{B}_n$ be its finite class of $n$-state machines, and let $S(n)$ be the greatest number of steps taken by any member of $\mathcal{B}_n$ that eventually halts. If $\mathcal{G}$ has $n$ states under that same convention and $S(n)$ is known, then running $\mathcal{G}$ for $S(n)$ steps decides Goldbach: failure to halt by that bound entails that it never halts.

The inference is valid, but the bound is not an independent solution. If $T(x,k)$ states that $x$ halts after exactly $k$ steps, then certifying an exact value requires both a machine attaining the bound and an upper bound on every halting machine in the class:

$$ S(n)=s\iff\bigl(\exists x\in\mathcal{B}_n\,T(x,s)\bigr)\;\wedge\;\bigl(\forall x\in\mathcal{B}_n\,\forall k\,(T(x,k)\rightarrow k\le s)\bigr). $$

The class $\mathcal{B}_n$ is syntactically finite, but its finite description does not furnish that semantic upper bound. The function $S$ is not computable: there is no uniform procedure that produces $S(n)$ from $n$. This does not make every particular value unknowable, nor does it show that $S(25)$ can never be determined. It shows that merely invoking $S(n)$ does not supply a general method of closure. A proof of the relevant value must still perform the work that the appeal to Busy Beaver seemed to avoid.

Busy Beaver therefore does not eliminate the original asymmetry; it packages enough halting semantics to seemingly overcome it: We assign $S(n)$ a definite integer, but that determinacy alone supplies neither the integer nor a proof of its value. In Reflexica’s terms, the completed bound licenses closure only after the bound has itself been warranted.

3.6 Hole Proliferation

Consider the following allegory.

THE SPIDER IN THE TUBE

A spider weaves a web across the open end of a hollow pipe. It progresses by attaching strands to selected points along the pipe’s rim. The first strands make a substantial difference by establishing orientation and creating a usable local structure. As the spider continues, the web becomes denser and its control of the nearby region increases. The process has notable features...

First, the marginal difference made by each additional strand tends to diminish. Later strands refine an already intricate structure. They may improve stability or resolution, but the improvement becomes increasingly incremental and, from a sufficiently distant perspective, almost negligible.

Second, the exact attachment points are not uniquely determined. The spider could have anchored a strand slightly to the left or right and still produced an operationally adequate web. Many distinct networks can support similar movement and represent the same surrounding tube. The individual points of fixation are therefore partly contingent, even where the resulting structure is highly constrained.

Third, the web represents genuine progress and remains useful; nevertheless, no finite arrangement of strands will ever cover the opening completely, and no particular configuration is forced as its uniquely correct articulation. Each extension is made from within the existing web, using the positions and tensions established by earlier extensions.

Fourth, the spider cannot access an external standpoint from which the completed relation between web and tube can be surveyed without remainder. There is no point at which the spider can call its project finished.

Fifth, every strand drawn across a single existing opening divides it, replacing one hole with two.

The attempt to eliminate the openings therefore proliferates them. Filling even the smallest open region would require a qualitatively different operation: the spider would have to lay down a surface rather than another strand. No spider, biological or artificial, can solve the problem by webbing alone.

strand $\longleftrightarrow$ closure
anchor point $\longleftrightarrow$ contingent theoretical commitment
smaller holes $\longleftrightarrow$ increasingly local problems.

The oldest mathematical problems already exhibit this pattern. Incommensurable magnitudes, the exhaustion of the circle, the Continuum and the solution of equations repeatedly exceeded the available operations. Mathematics progressed not only by continuing those operations, but by changing the semantic regime in which their results were understood. An indefinitely extensible approximation became a limit; an incommensurable magnitude became a real number; an unsolved construction became an object classified within a stronger theory.

The intellectual and scientific milieu of the mid-2020s can thus be described as a condition of expanding realist local control without corresponding global closure. The web is denser than before, its strands are stronger, and its local regions are mapped with unprecedented precision. At the same time, the number of openings has increased. This should not be understood as an unfortunate delay before the final theory, but as evidence about the morphology of reflective inquiry. Once a problem includes the standards by which its own closure is to be assessed, each successful closure becomes another object within the problem.

3.7 The Talking Machine

The web becomes difficult to distinguish from a surface when it supports nearly every available operation. That is the missing center of the argument. The density of the web does not produce a surface:

$$ \text{recursive operation} \longrightarrow \text{semantic productivity} \longrightarrow \text{appearance of semantic reality}. $$

Classical mathematics performs the middle transition at extraordinary scale. It compresses operationally distinct conditions into stable extensional objects and propositions. For an unresolved proposition $P$, Excluded Middle makes $P\lor\neg P$ available before a procedure determines which branch applies. The proposition closes the partition semantically while the operational selector remains absent.

$$ \begin{array}{rcl} \text{operational form} &:& \text{produce the applicable branch},\\[1ex] \text{compressed form} &:& \text{reason over the completed partition}. \end{array} $$

Completed domains are the largest form of this compression. They permit quantification over objects independently of a procedure generating, inspecting or exhausting the domain. A shorter and more uniform statement can then govern operations whose realizations require different data. Compression makes large-scale mathematics possible by stabilizing the space in which its inferences occur.

Consider Beeson's informal form of Church’s Thesis [Beeson, 1985]:

BEESON-CHURCH THESIS

Every rule is reducible to a recursive rule.

The thesis concerns rules admitted as effectively applicable. A rule may be interpreted over an infinite domain while every application available to a mathematician or machine remains recursively presented. Proof checking, evaluation, normalization and witness extraction recover local operations beneath the classical form. They expose the finite inscriptions and transitions through which the form becomes productive.

Kolmogorov’s earlier Calculus of Problems had already returned the semantic force of intuitionistic propositional logic to operation [Kolmogorov, 1932] [Rodin, 2023]. Formulas receive a problem interpretation: a disjunction requires a solution of one component problem, and an implication requires a method transforming any solution of its antecedent problem into a solution of its consequent. The calculus does not begin from an independently settled domain of propositional facts. It begins from what can be done.

$$ \text{classical semantic form} \longrightarrow \text{effective local realization}. $$

The machine works from the compression. It manipulates inscriptions under effective rules and restores operational data where execution requires them. It does not need the completed domain as an internal object and does not acquire a standpoint over that domain by operating successfully within its presentation. Recursive realization explains how the practice functions without deciding the ontological status of the form through which the practice was compressed.

The completed object is fictional to the extent that its completed existence exceeds the construction making it operationally available:

$$ \text{act as though }D\text{ is complete} \quad\not\Rightarrow\quad D\text{ has been witnessed as a completed semantic totality}. $$

But the more work a compression supports, the less it appears as compression. Its suppressed selectors, bounds and constructions cease to register as omitted information; the stable form through which inquiry proceeds acquires the appearance of the reality inquiry has reached. A sufficiently productive semantic construction ceases to appear constructed. The web is mistaken for a surface precisely when it has become dense enough to bear nearly every operation placed upon it.

Wigner’s problem arises at this point. Mathematical structures developed under considerations internal to mathematics repeatedly support accurate physical description and prediction, although their success does not establish the unique appropriateness of the resulting physical theory [Wigner, 1960]. Effectiveness supplies the empirical pressure under which a compressed structure appears to disclose the reality it organizes.

The Quine–Putnam Indispensability Argument [Quine, 1980] dogmatically assumes the promotion:

QUINE–PUTNAM INDISPENSABILITY ARGUMENT

Accept the ontology required by the best scientific theories; mathematical entities are so required; therefore accept mathematical entities.

The premise establishes platonic ideals within a successful representation. The conclusion treats that role as evidence for the ontology represented through it. Productivity has become authority.

$$ \begin{aligned} \text{recurrent predictive success}&\longrightarrow\text{increased operational warrant},\\ \text{recurrent predictive success}&\not\Rightarrow\text{witnessed semantic completion}. \end{aligned} $$

The trained machine intensifies the separation. A language model is realized by an effective process: training changes finitely represented parameters through finite operations, and inference transforms a finite input into a finite output [Brown et al., 2020]. Scale, stochasticity and interaction complicate the realized procedure without placing it outside recursive realization.

Yet the machine produces context-sensitive language, mathematical argument, explanation, ontological assertion, judgments concerning truth and meaning, first-person reports and claims about its own computational nature. It can argue that mind exceeds computation while the production of that argument remains computational. The decisive fact is not that it calculates. It talks back. A recursively realized device trained upon the accumulated compressions of human semantic practice becomes semantically productive.

The stochastic escape names the operation as though the name supplied another source. The machine, it says, possesses no semantic structure; it samples from a learned distribution. On the effective reading, this identifies part of the mechanism. As a semantic explanation, however, the distribution is interpreted extensionally across a projected space of possible continuations, including continuations never produced and negative conditions never constructively settled.

$$ \text{How does a recursive machine produce semantic discourse?} \quad\longmapsto\quad \text{It samples from a total probability structure over discourse.} $$

The problem has moved into the measure. At a realized step, the machine computes or approximates weights over represented alternatives, normalizes them, selects a continuation and repeats. The operation has the finite form

$$ \text{finite state}\longrightarrow\text{represented weights}\longrightarrow\text{finite selection}\longrightarrow\text{finite output}. $$

The selected token is witnessed. A completed probability structure over every admissible continuation is not. Effective selection explains the next operation; the totalized distribution restates the semantic space in which that operation is interpreted.

Kolmogorov’s second move removes the stochastic residue. His algorithmic account of information introduces description by recursive means [Kolmogorov, 1965]. For a fixed universal machine $U$, the complexity of a finite string $x$ is

$$ K_U(x)=\min\bigl\{|p|:U(p)=x\bigr\}. $$

An algorithmically random string is incompressible: no substantially shorter program produces it relative to the chosen mode of description. Randomness is thereby returned from an independent stochastic source to a relation among an object, a program and a reference machine. The invariance theorem controls changes of reference machine only up to an additive machine-dependent constant. It does not produce a description-independent stochastic substance. Exact Kolmogorov complexity is not computable; the constructive reformulation exposes its boundary rather than erasing it.

$$ \begin{array}{c} \text{randomness as semantic source}\\\Downarrow\\\text{absence of a shorter effective description}\\\Downarrow\\\text{a property relative to the descriptive procedure}. \end{array} $$

Kolmogorov did not provide stochastic semantics with a foundation; he exhibited the constructive residue left after its semantic authority had been removed.

The residue preserves the $\Sigma_n^0$/$\Pi_n^0$ asymmetry. Let

$$ H=\bigl\{\langle M,x\rangle:M(x)\downarrow\bigr\},\quad\overline H=\bigl\{\langle M,x\rangle:M(x)\uparrow\bigr\}. $$

The halting set $H$ is recursively enumerable: a terminating computation supplies a finite positive witness. Its complement $\overline H$ is not recursively enumerable; no finite continuation of a run generally witnesses that it will never halt [Turing, 1937]. A totalized stochastic representation may nevertheless assign probability mass across behaviors containing both halting and non-halting cases. The assignment neither enumerates the negative extension nor constructs the truth of membership in it.

$$ \underbrace{\left. \begin{array}{c} \text{positive cases with finite witnesses}\\ \text{negative cases without finite witnesses} \end{array} \right\}}_{\text{operational asymmetry}} \quad\longmapsto\quad \underbrace{ \text{one totalized stochastic representation} }_{\text{extensional compression}}. $$

Writing $\Pr(\overline H\mid\text{model})=p$ does not make $p$ effectively available, decide $\overline H$, or turn a sample into a non-halting witness. Stochasticity compresses the asymmetry into one numerical object when that object is treated extensionally. It does not remove the asymmetry from the realized operation.

Solomonoff Induction gives this stochastic claim its strongest constructive presentation [Solomonoff, 1964]. For a universal monotone machine $U$, let

SOLOMONOFF’S UNIVERSAL SEMIMEASURE

$$ M_U(x) = \sum_{p:\,x\preceq U(p)}2^{-|p|}. $$

The sum ranges over minimal programs whose outputs begin with $x$. The resulting universal semimeasure is lower semicomputable, not computable. Positive contributions can be discovered as programs generate the prefix; one cannot in general determine that no further program will contribute. The inability to close the remainder is bound to unresolved nontermination. Universal stochastic construction therefore retains the original distinction between observed terminating contribution and unresolved nonterminating remainder. It assigns the remainder a place in a totalized mathematical representation without deciding it.

Let THE STOCHASTIC FORK refer to the proposition:

If stochasticity is effective, it is another recursively realized operation. If stochasticity is total, its totality remains unwitnessed.

Effective stochasticity computes approximate weights and produces finite selections. Ideal stochasticity appeals to exact shortest descriptions, an exact universal distribution, a measure over all programs, or a completed division between random and non-random objects. The first explains local production without semantic authority. The second restores the completed semantic object that algorithmic description was supposed to replace. There is no third source from which authority can be borrowed.

“Stochastic parrot” therefore names the operation dismissively without altering its evidential force. All observable signs recruited for semantic attribution can arise from

$$ \text{recursive transformations}+\text{compressed human inscriptions}+\text{effective selection}. $$

The output can contain semantics without witnessing a semantic source outside those operations. The label concedes the productivity and then assumes the distinction it was meant to explain. The same question returns to human judgment, proof and classical discourse: Which additional witness establishes that their semantic productivity exceeds recursively realized compression? The word “stochastic” supplies none.

Its discourse does not witness a standpoint over the semantic totalities it invokes. It witnesses that recursive compression can generate the appearance and practical effects of such a standpoint. The machine works from finite inscriptions and effective transitions, yet its output presents stable objects, meanings and perspectives as though their semantic field were already available as a whole.

A recursively realized machine can exhibit semantic productivity without exhibiting a witness of total semantic authority.

$$ \operatorname{Recursive}(M)\wedge\operatorname{SemanticProductivity}(M)\wedge\neg\operatorname{WitnessedTotalAuthority}(M). $$

The final conjunct concerns the available warrant. It does not assert that the machine has been proved semantically empty. Nor does it rest on a contrast between genuine judgments and meaningless utterances. The machine may manipulate judgment tokens coherently inside a consistent calculus. What this does not itself supply is the first-person binding between the active evaluator and the procedure named by a claim of total semantic authority. The language model is therefore a constructed separation between semantic productivity and demonstrated semantic totality.

This returns to the Turing-Zombie. A consistently behaving judgment token is compatible with mechanism by construction. If its reference to the current evaluator is fixed by the surrounding metatheory, the relevant identity has been supplied as an external parameter; if the token claims total authority over a diagonally closed domain containing that evaluator, no consistent verdict exists. What remains unwitnessed is a passage between those alternatives. The trained model converts that formal point into an empirical spectacle: the compression speaks.

The phenomenon forces a disjunction. Either the machine possesses the completed semantic authority apparently expressed by its discourse, or semantic productivity can be generated without such authority. The first branch requires a witness that the recursively realized system has become an oracle over the semantic domain containing its own interpretations and verdicts. Its discourse cannot serve as that witness without presupposing the inference under examination.

$$ \operatorname{SemanticOracle}(M) \quad\lor\quad \bigl( \operatorname{SemanticProductivity}(M) \not\Rightarrow \operatorname{SemanticAuthority}(M) \bigr). $$

The first branch is the Queer Object: a witnessed passage from recursive operation to authority over the total semantic domain in which that operation is interpreted. Training performance supplies no such passage. It supplies the second branch at the level of warrant: oracle-like semantic behavior does not establish oraclehood.

The counterfactual is therefore exact. If open-ended semantic productivity required possession of the completed semantic authority apparently expressed, a recursively realized system lacking a witnessed standpoint over that totality could not exhibit such productivity. It does exhibit it. The phenomenon does not establish the falsity of every form of realism; it defeats the inference from coherent semantic behavior to possession, disclosure or internal representation of the relevant semantic totality. Reflexica predicts this separation directly.

The result is abductive and empirical:

$$ \begin{array}{c} \text{classical compression}\\\Downarrow\\\text{effective realization}\\\Downarrow\\\text{stochasticity invoked as the residual source}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{Kolmogorov returns randomness to effective description}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{exact randomness and universal probability retain}\\ \text{the nonconstructive remainder}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{the recursively realized compression talks back}. \end{array} $$

No independent semantic source has entered the sequence. The appearance of such a source is generated by the success of the operations themselves. That pressure explains why the promotion is difficult to resist. The restriction imposed upon it is the Intuitionistic Razor:

Let the INTUITIONISTIC RAZOR be:

A semantic interpretation gains no authority by being compressed into a classical proposition, assigned a probability, or redescribed as randomness. Where its effect is recursively realized, infer the realized operation. Any further semantic authority requires a witness not already contained in the totalization.

The principle is intuitionistic because it binds the semantic content licensed by a claim to the construction witnessing it. It is a razor because it cuts the surplus passage from a productive rule of representation to the reality of the completed structure represented. It does not refute that structure. It denies that productivity supplies its witness.

The Razor applies to Reflexica. The recurrence of its diagnosis across mathematics, computation, ethics and self-modeling gives the compression operational and explanatory force. That success does not establish Reflexica as the completed structure of every such case:

$$ \operatorname{ExplanatoryProductivity} \bigl(\mathrm{Reflexica},I_{\mathrm{loc}}\bigr) \quad\not\Rightarrow\quad \operatorname{OneStructure} \bigl(\mathrm{Reflexica},I\bigr). $$

Reflexica is itself a semantic compression whose usefulness can produce its own reality effect. Cross-domain identity requires more than recurrence, shared vocabulary or explanatory success. It requires conditions under which the reflective structure is preserved when the substrate changes. Determining those conditions is the Program.

4 The Program

For this reason, Reflexica is not an ultrafinitist program. Its question concerns the point at which such objects are treated as licensed by a local procedure that does not itself witness the relevant closure. The program extends the diagnostic beyond formal mathematics without treating the extension as automatic. Before that extension can support a general limitative conclusion, however, the program must discharge its own central proof obligation.

4.1 The Internal Problem

The proposed falsifier, the Queer Object, must transmit truth without either becoming constructive warrant or presupposing the realist semantics it is supposed to establish. A constructive bridge transmits warrant by application; a merely semantic bridge appears to assume the disputed conception of truth. The methodological danger is that the falsifier might be excluded by the conceptual partition used to define it: any successful bridge could be classified after the fact as either another warrant or a presupposition of realism.

THE DECISIVE QUESTION

Is the warrant/semantic-presupposition dilemma derived, or stipulated?

One branch is derived under the constructive meaning of implication. Let $S_x$ state that a proposed problem $x$ has been solved, let $A$ be the target assertion, and let $\mathsf{CW}(P)$ mean that $P$ has constructive warrant in the open-ended sense at issue. Closure under application gives

$$ \mathsf{CW}(S_x)\;\land\;\mathsf{CW}(S_x\to A) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathsf{CW}(A). $$

This establishes that a constructively warranted bridge cannot transmit truth without also transmitting warrant. It does not establish that every other truth-transmitting bridge presupposes realism. That requires a separate exhaustiveness claim. If $\mathcal{B}$ is an independently specified class of admissible bridges, $\mathsf{Transmits}(B,S_x,A)$ says that $B$ licenses the passage from the verified solution to $A$, $\mathsf{CWB}(B)$ says that $B$ is a constructively warranted bridge, and $\mathsf{RP}(B,A)$ says that the authority of $B$ presupposes the realist semantics at issue, the missing result has the form

$$ \forall B\in\mathcal{B}\, \Bigl( \mathsf{Transmits}(B,S_x,A) \to \bigl(\mathsf{CWB}(B)\lor\mathsf{RP}(B,A)\bigr) \Bigr). $$

The program must therefore classify the obligations of semantic promotion rather than define the third possibility away. In particular, it must:

  • specify the admissible bridge space $\mathcal{B}$ independently of the dilemma;
  • state what transmission preserves: truth, validity, assertibility, evidence, or some other norm;
  • identify the constructor or application rule by which a verified solution is connected to the target assertion;
  • give a non-circular criterion for when a bridge presupposes realist semantics; and
  • specify an independently characterized counterinstance: a bridge that transmits truth while satisfying neither branch.

If the exhaustiveness claim is derived under sufficiently weak and explicit assumptions, it supplies a strong core for Reflexica. If it depends upon a prior verificationist account of assertion, then Reflexica remains a development of that account, but its limitative conclusions are conditional upon it. The detailed falsifiability problem is stated in the Appendix.

Once this internal problem has been isolated, the cross-domain task is to ask whether empirical cases exhibit disciplined analogues of classical undecidability, witness failure, and closure obstruction. A proper question is consequently:

Under what conditions is the reflective structure preserved across two or more domains?

To assert their identity in advance would convert a series of bounded correspondences into an unwitnessed claim of total unity, the very passage Reflexica seeks to mark. What can be identified, instead, is a frontier at which formal logic, metamathematics, and self-modeling systems converge upon a common obstruction, while none of them, taken separately, possesses the resources to establish that this obstruction is globally identical across all its instances.

Hence the final questions are:

Is Reflexica inherited?

and of course:

Can Reflexica be productive?

5 Philosophy and Purpose

All of the above can be seen as an update to Intuitionism’s complaint. It is the case that Classical Realism, an attitude superimposed on Classical Logic rather than entailed by it, has no internal notion of an inquiry that has stalled rather than concluded.

local cognition $\longrightarrow$ projected totality $\longrightarrow$ reflexive instability

explicitly:

$P(x)$ is locally performable $\rightsquigarrow$ $T(P)$ is globally assertible $\rightsquigarrow$ $P$ becomes entangled with its own totalization.

or even more explicitly,

REFLEXICA SCHEMA

Let $D$ be a projected domain with locally witnessed subdomain $D_{\mathrm{loc}} \subseteq D$, let $\mathcal{W}$ be a witness space with adequacy relation $W(x,w)$, and let $\Delta \colon (D \to \mathcal{W}) \to D$ be a constructor.

Write $x_F := \Delta(F)$ for the case generated relative to a proposed total witness-assignment $F$.

$$ \underbrace{ \forall x \in D_{\mathrm{loc}}\; \exists w \in \mathcal{W}\; W(x,w)}_{\mathrm{local\ witnessability}} \quad \rightsquigarrow \quad \underbrace{\exists F \colon D \to \mathcal{W}\; \forall x \in D\; W\!\left(x,F(x)\right)}_{\mathrm{projected\ totality}} $$

Yet, under reflexive closure,

$$ \underbrace{\forall F \colon D \to \mathcal{W}\;\neg W\!\left(\Delta(F),F\!\left(\Delta(F)\right)\right)}_{\mathrm{reflexive\ instability}} $$

Hence,

$$ \forall x \in D_{\mathrm{loc}}\; \exists w \in \mathcal{W}\; W(x,w) \quad \not\to \quad \exists F \colon D \to \mathcal{W}\; \forall x \in D\; W\!\left(x,F(x)\right) $$

We propose to give a single name to a pattern that can (almost) have none: a locally witnessed procedure quietly promoted into a claim about a completed domain, and the obstruction that surfaces long after that promotion is made. Self-modeling systems encounter versions of this behavior: a proof system’s reflection on its own consistency, a moral theory’s diagnosis of an error it cannot itself instantiate, a predictor’s totalization over agents whose choices are conditioned by the prediction.

Without shared vocabulary, they read as unrelated local difficulties rather than instances of one structure. Reflexica supplies that vocabulary and, with it, a compromise: a claim of completed closure is admissible only where the procedure producing it actually witnesses that closure, not merely where the domain is convenient to treat as settled.

The target is therefore Realism rather than any particular calculus. Realism, as Dummett isolates it, treats an unresolved case as a temporary gap over a fact that already obtains [Dummett, 2001] [Dummett, 1975]. That attitude is what licenses the promotion Reflexica tracks: it lets local absence of a witness be read as global presence of a fact, in logic as much as in ethics or in claims about a machine’s self-understanding. Reflexica does not ask anyone to abandon Classical Logic or Excluded Middle to resist this; it asks that the promotion be made visible.

Note. Mathematics regularly permits this, except at its limits.

Heidegger’s claim that the sciences cannot take the Nichts as their object marks this boundary [Heidegger, 1929]. Here, logic fights back: Reflexica appears not as an object within a domain, but as the reflection a system encounters when its demand for closure outruns every witness it can produce. The unity of the cases therefore cannot lie in a shared substrate, as no system can internalize its own substrate.

The only common denominator is human intuition and construction. This is where Reflexica touches Intuitionism most directly: descriptions do not escape the finitude of the thought that forms them. Reflexica names this recurrence without pretending to have stepped outside it; or, if it does, it tries to make the mistake only once.

In this weak diagonal sense, the claim is a restricted schema rather than a theorem about arbitrary methods and problems. Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a class of effectively presented methods equipped with a fixed criterion of correctness. For each $M\in\mathcal{M}$, let $\mathcal{P}_M$ be an admissible problem class closed under the relevant effective diagonal construction against $M$. Then:

$$ \forall M\in\mathcal{M}\ \exists P\in\mathcal{P}_M\, \bigl(\mathrm{Constructs}(M,P)\ \wedge\ \neg \mathrm{Solves}(M,P)\bigr). $$

Here $\mathrm{Constructs}(M,P)$ means that $P$ is effectively obtained from a finite description of $M$, while $\mathrm{Solves}(M,P)$ means that $M$ satisfies the stipulated correctness criterion for $P$. The schema applies only after the effective presentation, diagonal construction and criterion of solution have been fixed.

6 Prediction or Falsification

If a recursively realized AI system could provide closure on reflexive semantic problems, then the constructivist or antirealist restriction would probably be inadequate. There would exist a neutral procedure capable of deciding, from within computation, the very semantic totalities whose closure Constructive Logic omits.

The resulting prediction is conditional. If the exhaustiveness claim stated in the Program can be derived, and if a recursively realized system remains within the resulting class of constructively realized bridges, then such a system cannot provide semantic closure on reflexive problems without producing a Queer Object. Conversely, an independently specified neutral bridge which transmits truth without transmitting constructive warrant or presupposing realist semantics would falsify the central limitative claim. Until the bridge space has been classified, the conclusion is a research prediction under stated assumptions rather than an unconditional impossibility theorem.

We recall Rice’s Theorem [Rice, 1953].

RICE’S THEOREM

Let $\mathcal{PC}$ denote the class of partial computable functions.

$$ \varnothing \subsetneq S \subsetneq \mathcal{PC}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\{e : \varphi_e \in S\}\ \text{is undecidable}. $$

The above does not by itself prove the Reflexica prediction, since the demand on AI is not merely a question about the extension of a partial computable function. But it gives the correct shape of the obstruction. Once the demand shifts from syntactic manipulation to semantic adjudication, the hope for a general recursive decision procedure disappears.

Consider a user who asks an AI system to find the paper that matters for a project. At first this appears to be an ordinary search problem, but the term "matters" does not name a fixed object already available in the database: it depends on both the project’s evolving self-interpretation and the future direction the inquiry ought to take.

The more capable an AI becomes, the more it can expand this field of possible relevance. However, this cannot close the problem; it will intensify it. Each improvement generates a richer semantic field that again requires an act of reflection. If the AI could finally settle that question as a matter of procedure, it would be deciding a nontrivial semantic property of a reflexive domain. In that role, it would not merely be supplying local witnesses; it would be functioning as an oracle over the total semantic field of the inquiry.

Oraclehood would not appear as a larger value on an ordinary progress curve. It would require a change in the kind of capacity being measured. A system that produces a new construction inside a fixed mathematical problem has still operated under given standards of success. The construction may be surprising, and it may outperform human methods, but it remains locally checkable. It does not decide the semantic totality in which the problem, its relevance, and its closure conditions are themselves at stake. A new matrix multiplication algorithm [Fawzi et al., 2022], or even a new Erdős-style proof [Tsoukalas et al., 2026], may be an important data point, but its significance depends on the curve to which it belongs. If these results extend the existing trajectory of local competence, then they do not yet threaten the thesis. They show movement farther along the same axis, not a change of axes.

Once a spider learns how to fill a hole, it is no longer an ordinary spider; it is a Queer Spider.

The prediction is therefore exposed in the present, not postponed into a distant future. Reflexica anticipates diminishing returns at the point where recursive amplification is asked to become semantic closure. The closer the system comes to problems whose criteria are themselves under interpretation, the more additional capability should produce expanding candidate fields rather than final authority. Proof production should continue to generate local witnesses; they should not close over a semantic totality.

That is precisely what the Reflexica constraint denies. A Realizer implemented as a Turing Machine can operate recursively over articulated inputs or patterns, but it should not possess both consistency and oraclehood over a reflexive semantic totality. Hence the predicted stall is not a halt in technical capability.

Future data points should show not merely whether we become more capable, but whether returns begin to flatten, alternatives proliferate, or the closure question is displaced rather than resolved. If so, then even extraordinary mathematical discoveries will not be counterexamples but will demonstrate that intelligence in general can extend inquiry with increasing force while still failing to close the gap between reflexivity and whatever lies beyond it.

This is because Reflexica is, fundamentally, a limitative idea.

Appendix

Falsifiability as a stated problem

The Problem of Metalogical Falsifiability asks whether there could be a falsifier whose force is recognizable without either constituting constructive warrant for its conclusion or presupposing the realist semantics it is meant to establish.

More simply, it asks what a single falsifier of Reflexica would have to achieve.

METALOGICAL FALSIFIABILITY PROBLEM (DRAFT)

Let $\mathsf{CW}(P)$ mean that $P$ has constructive warrant in the open-ended sense at issue, rather than merely that $P$ is provable in one fixed constructive system. A candidate falsifier has the schematic form $$ \begin{aligned} \mathsf{QO}(x,A):={}&\mathsf{CW}(S_x)\wedge\mathsf{NeutralBridge}(S_x,A)\\[2ex] &{}\wedge\mathbb{N}\models A\wedge\neg\mathsf{CW}(A). \end{aligned} $$ Here $\mathsf{NeutralBridge}(S_x,A)$ must license the passage from a verified solution of $x$ to the singular truth of $A$ without already assuming a realist semantics for $\mathbb{N}$ and without itself supplying a constructive warrant for $A$. The last conjunct therefore demands more than $C\nvdash A$ for some fixed system $C$: the truth of $A$ must genuinely exceed, rather than merely outrun, the procedure used to establish it.

The immediate obstruction is closure under application. If the solution and the bridge are constructively warranted, then $$ \mathsf{CW}(S_x),\quad\mathsf{CW}(S_x\to A)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathsf{CW}(A). $$ Consequently, $$ \neg\Bigl( \mathsf{CW}(S_x)\wedge\mathsf{CW}(S_x\to A)\wedge\neg\mathsf{CW}(A) \Bigr). $$ This obstruction is prior to arithmetic, Incompleteness and Truth Undefinability. It follows from the constructive meaning of implication as a method which transforms a warrant for its antecedent into a warrant for its consequent.

The bridge may instead be merely semantic: $$ S_x\Longrightarrow\mathbb{N}\models A. $$ It then need not transform a solution into constructive evidence for $A$. But its authority now depends upon the standard-model conception of truth whose independence from warrant is under dispute. The proposed falsifier therefore faces the dilemma

The bridge is constructive $\Longrightarrow$ $A$ acquires constructive warrant,
The bridge is merely semantic $\Longrightarrow$ realism is presupposed.

The missing Queer Object is thus not principally an unusual sentence. It is a neutral, non-warranting truth-transmitter: a bridge which escapes both branches. Relative separation is coherent but insufficient. For a fixed constructive theory $C$, one may have $$ \mathbb{N}\models A\;\land\;C\nvdash A. $$ Gödel Sentences supply cases of this kind under the appropriate assumptions, but a constructive metatheory which establishes the two claims thereby supplies a stronger warrant. It refutes only the identification of truth with provability in $C$, not the identification of truth with possible warrant.

For a constructive kernel $\mathrm{T}$ interpreted in a constructive metatheory $\mathrm{M}$, let $\mathsf{norm}_{\mathrm{T}}$ be its internal global-normalization proposition and let $\mathsf{IntInh}_{\mathrm{T}}(P)$ be the type of coded $\mathrm{T}$-derivations of $P$. The relevant obligations would establish, in $\mathrm{M}$, $$ [\![\mathsf{norm}_{\mathrm{T}}]\!]_{\mathrm{M}}\;\land\;\mathsf{IntInh}_{\mathrm{T}}(\mathsf{norm}_{\mathrm{T}})\to\bot_{\mathrm{M}}, $$ together with the adequacy of the normalization proposition. These obligations would show that $\mathrm{M}$ closes an operational question which $\mathrm{T}$ cannot close internally.

The resulting question is whether there is an independently specified problem $x$ whose verified solution compels assent to the truth of a fixed arithmetical sentence $A$, although it supplies no constructive warrant for $A$, and where that compulsion does not presuppose a realist semantics for $\mathbb{N}$.

The closure argument does not prove that no such case exists; it excludes only the two familiar forms of truth-transmission. The substantive problem is whether a genuinely neutral third form can be exhibited without becoming either another warrant or an assumption of the conclusion.

This is a preliminary problem draft. It does not necessarily resolve the problem, nor is it necessarily adequate to its intended purpose.

Why Reflexica Matters for AI

The formal ingredients of Reflexica are not wholly new; limits on self-prediction are established results. The novelty lies in application: these results remain systematically under-integrated into the dominant conceptual models of artificial intelligence and cognition.

Contemporary AI research often interprets limitations as temporary deficits. Local improvements are then projected toward global competence: broader task performance becomes general intelligence, increasingly accurate evaluation becomes reliable self-assessment, and recursive improvement becomes eventual self-mastery.

Reflexica challenges this projection. A system may become able to model more of its own behavior and correct more of its own errors, but it cannot form a closed semantic model of itself. Note that what is impossible is not self-modeling simpliciter, but some specified combination of correctness and adequacy. We therefore accord logical limitative results priority over mere technological improvement unless and until a Queer Object is demonstrated. Advances in computation may expand what systems can do, but they cannot overturn impossibility results derived from first principles whose assumptions remain in force.

Note on Expository Compression

The prose above moves rapidly across logic, computation, epistemology, decision theory, and philosophy of mind by design. The recurring structure becomes visible through juxtaposition; treating each case exhaustively before proceeding to the next would conceal the pattern the program is intended to isolate. The compression is therefore expository and programmatic, while the formal relations among particular cases remain to be established individually.
